My experience in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, has been a defining part of my professional development as a physician and public health practitioner. Working in a complex humanitarian setting that serves both the Rohingya refugee population and the surrounding host community has provided me with firsthand experience of how displacement, poverty, gender, environmental vulnerability, limited resources, and health-system constraints interact to shape population health.

This experience has strengthened my commitment to health equity, humanitarian health, resilient health systems, and evidence-based interventions for vulnerable populations. More importantly, it has influenced the questions I seek to answer through public health research and my aspiration to pursue doctoral training.

01 — Population Health

Rohingya Refugee & Host-Community Health

Working within the health system in Cox's Bazar has exposed me to the complex healthcare needs of displaced populations alongside those of the host community. The coexistence of humanitarian and routine health needs requires coordinated approaches that address immediate health priorities while strengthening sustainable healthcare delivery.

My professional experience has involved supporting health services and public health activities related to maternal and child health, adolescent and reproductive health, vaccination, disease prevention, surveillance, health education, community engagement, and health-system coordination.

Humanitarian health cannot be separated from broader questions of health systems, equity, social determinants, and long-term resilience.

This setting has strengthened my understanding that humanitarian health requires both immediate responses to urgent needs and longer-term approaches to sustainable and equitable healthcare delivery.

02 — Women & Adolescents

Maternal, Reproductive & Adolescent Health

Women and adolescents in humanitarian settings often face multiple barriers to accessing timely and appropriate healthcare. My work has therefore included engagement with maternal, reproductive, and adolescent health programs, with particular attention to improving access to preventive and essential health services.

These experiences have strengthened my interest in understanding how healthcare systems can better respond to the needs of women and adolescents affected by displacement and vulnerability.

03 — Disease Prevention

Vaccination & Disease Prevention

Vaccination is an important component of humanitarian health, where population displacement, overcrowding, mobility, misinformation, and barriers to healthcare access can increase vulnerability to vaccine-preventable diseases.

Through my professional work, I have gained practical insight into the importance of immunization, health communication, community engagement, surveillance, and coordination in supporting disease prevention.

💉

Immunization

Understanding vaccination as an essential population-level strategy for preventing disease and promoting health equity.

📢

Health Communication

Recognizing the importance of clear, trusted, and context-sensitive communication in vaccination programs.

🤝

Community Engagement

Working with communities and stakeholders to improve awareness, trust, and preventive health practices.

🧠

Vaccine Hesitancy

Exploring how perceptions, parental influence, social norms, trust, and health-system factors affect acceptance.

These experiences have strengthened my interest in vaccination research, particularly HPV vaccine hesitancy among adolescents. I am interested in understanding how individual perceptions, parental influence, social norms, communication, trust, and health-system factors affect vaccine acceptance in different populations.

08 — Research Perspective

From Humanitarian Practice to Research

My humanitarian experience has fundamentally influenced the way I think about public health research. Field practice has shown me that health problems are rarely caused by a single factor.

They emerge from the interaction of individual behavior, social determinants, environmental conditions, health-system capacity, policy, and structural inequality.

Field Observation

Direct exposure to complex health challenges affecting vulnerable populations.

Research Questions

Identifying gaps between health needs, policy, program design, and implementation.

Evidence Generation

Applying rigorous research methods to understand determinants and evaluate interventions.

Translation to Action

Using evidence to inform programs, policy, implementation, and community practice.

I see humanitarian settings not simply as places where health services are delivered, but also as important environments for generating research questions and developing innovative, context-sensitive solutions.

Long-Term Global Health Vision

During doctoral training, I aim to investigate interventions that can improve vaccination uptake, maternal and child health, adolescent health, and health-system resilience, particularly among populations experiencing social, environmental, or humanitarian vulnerability.

My long-term goal is to contribute to a global health research career that connects field experience, rigorous science, policy, and implementation.

I hope to work collaboratively with academic institutions, governments, communities, and international organizations to generate evidence that can improve health outcomes and strengthen resilient health systems in Bangladesh and other low- and middle-income settings.

A Commitment to Vulnerable Populations

My humanitarian health experience has reinforced a commitment that remains central to my professional identity: public health research should be accountable to the people and communities it seeks to serve.

The experiences I have gained in Cox's Bazar have not only strengthened my clinical and public health competencies but have also shaped the research questions I want to pursue, the populations I want to serve, and the kind of researcher I aspire to become.
