Field & Community Work

Bridging Public Health Research and Community Practice

Connecting field experience, community engagement, humanitarian health practice, and evidence-based public health research.

Community Engagement Health Campaigns Training Public Health Programs Humanitarian Health Research Questions Doctoral Research Field Activities Ethical Commitment Impact

My field and community-based work has been an essential part of my development as a public health professional and emerging researcher. Working directly with communities has allowed me to understand how social, cultural, environmental, and health-system factors shape health behaviors and access to essential services. These experiences have strengthened my belief that impactful public health research must remain connected to the realities of the communities it seeks to serve.

Through my professional and humanitarian health work in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, I have engaged with diverse populations, including Rohingya refugees and host communities, and contributed to activities related to maternal and child health, adolescent and reproductive health, vaccination, disease prevention, health education, community engagement, and health-system coordination.

01 — Community Practice

Community Engagement

Meaningful community engagement is central to my approach to public health. I have participated in activities that bring healthcare providers, adolescents, families, community representatives, and other stakeholders together to identify health needs, improve awareness, and strengthen connections between communities and health services.

These experiences have helped me appreciate the importance of understanding community perceptions, social norms, trust, and local contexts when designing public health interventions.

"Effective public health interventions must be grounded in community realities, trust, local knowledge, and cultural context."

They have also directly informed my research interest in HPV vaccine hesitancy, where community and stakeholder perspectives are essential for understanding vaccination decisions and developing culturally responsive strategies.

05 — Humanitarian Practice

Humanitarian Health Experience

Working at the Intersection of Vulnerability, Health & Resilience

My experience working in a humanitarian setting has been particularly influential in shaping my research interests. Delivering and coordinating health services in a context affected by displacement, vulnerability, environmental risks, and resource constraints has provided me with firsthand insight into the complexity of health inequities.

These experiences have strengthened my interest in humanitarian health, refugee health, health-system resilience, climate-related health risks, and equitable access to preventive and essential healthcare.

They have also motivated me to pursue research that moves beyond describing health problems toward identifying interventions that are feasible, scalable, and responsive to the needs of vulnerable populations.

06 — Research Perspective

From Field Experience to Research Questions

My field experience continually informs the questions I ask as a researcher. Interactions with communities and healthcare providers have helped me recognize gaps between health policy, program design, and implementation.

Vaccination & Adolescents

My work with adolescents and vaccination-related activities contributed to my interest in understanding why evidence-based vaccines may not be accepted even when they are available.

Trust, Communication & Social Norms

I am interested in how communication, trust, social norms, parental influence, and health-system factors shape vaccination decisions.

Climate, Displacement & Health

Working in a climate-vulnerable and humanitarian setting has strengthened my interest in understanding how environmental change, displacement, and health-system vulnerability interact.

Maternal, Child & Reproductive Health

These experiences have encouraged me to explore how environmental and health-system challenges influence maternal, child, and reproductive health outcomes.

I view field experience and research as complementary: field experience generates meaningful research questions, while rigorous research can provide evidence for improving field practice and policy.

11 — Impact & Future Vision

Connecting Practice, Research & Impact

Research That Ultimately Serves Communities

My field experience has reinforced a central principle of my professional journey: public health research should ultimately serve communities.

I hope to continue building a career in which clinical understanding, field experience, rigorous research, and policy engagement work together to address health inequities.

Through doctoral training and future research collaborations, I aim to transform lessons from the field into scientifically rigorous evidence and, ultimately, into interventions and policies that improve health outcomes for women, children, adolescents, refugees, and other vulnerable populations.

From Communities to Evidence

My field and community experiences continue to shape how I understand public health problems, formulate research questions, and envision meaningful interventions. They have taught me that strong public health research requires both scientific rigor and a deep understanding of the people and systems it seeks to serve.
