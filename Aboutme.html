About Me

Dr. Nasrin Jebin, MBBS, MPH

Physician  |  Public Health Researcher  |  Humanitarian Health Professional

I am Dr. Nasrin Jebin (MBBS, MPH), a physician and public health researcher dedicated to advancing equitable healthcare through evidence-based research, clinical practice, and humanitarian service. My professional journey has been shaped by a commitment to improving the health and well-being of vulnerable populations, particularly women, adolescents, and children. With interdisciplinary training in medicine and public health, I strive to bridge clinical care, research, and health policy to address some of today's most pressing global health challenges.

Education & Professional Foundation

I earned my Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) from Sir Salimullah Medical College, Bangladesh, where I developed a strong foundation in clinical medicine and patient-centered care. Recognizing that many health challenges extend beyond hospital settings, I pursued a Master of Public Health (MPH) at the Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP).

My graduate education strengthened my expertise in epidemiology, biostatistics, health systems, implementation research, and health policy, while deepening my understanding of population health and preventive medicine.

Humanitarian & Clinical Experience

My current professional role as a Health Focal/Medical Officer at Ukhia Upazila Health Complex, Cox's Bazar, has provided invaluable experience working in one of the world's largest humanitarian settings. Serving both the host community and Rohingya refugees has enabled me to contribute to maternal and child health services, adolescent health programs, reproductive health initiatives, disease surveillance, outbreak preparedness, vaccination activities, and health system coordination.

Working in this resource-constrained environment has strengthened my ability to deliver quality healthcare while collaborating with government agencies, international organizations, and local partners.

Research Interests

Beyond clinical responsibilities, I have developed a strong interest in public health research that informs policy and practice. My current research focuses on HPV vaccination hesitancy among adolescent girls in Bangladesh, examining the knowledge, attitudes, behavioral determinants, and contextual factors influencing vaccine acceptance.

Through quantitative and qualitative research methods, I aim to generate evidence that supports effective vaccination strategies and contributes to the prevention of cervical cancer. I am particularly interested in implementation science approaches that improve vaccine confidence, strengthen community engagement, and reduce health inequities.

Climate Change & Health

My broader research interests also include maternal and child health, adolescent reproductive health, and the health impacts of climate change. Climate change is increasingly affecting maternal nutrition, pregnancy outcomes, infectious disease transmission, and healthcare delivery, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.

I am interested in investigating how climate-related risks interact with existing social and health inequalities and in developing evidence-based, climate-resilient interventions that protect vulnerable populations. I believe that integrating environmental health, reproductive health, and health systems research is essential for building resilient and sustainable healthcare systems.

Humanitarian Service & Leadership

Humanitarian service has profoundly influenced my professional values. Working with displaced and underserved populations has reinforced the importance of equity, cultural sensitivity, community participation, and evidence-informed decision-making.

These experiences have strengthened my leadership, multidisciplinary collaboration, and problem-solving skills while highlighting the critical role of public health research in addressing complex humanitarian and global health challenges.

Career Vision

My long-term career vision is to become an internationally recognized researcher and academic leader in public and global health, conducting high-impact research that translates into effective policies and scalable interventions.

I aspire to collaborate with universities, governments, and international organizations to improve vaccination programs, maternal and child health services, and climate-resilient health systems in resource-limited settings. Alongside research, I hope to mentor future public health professionals and contribute to strengthening research capacity in Bangladesh and other low- and middle-income countries.

Why a PhD?

Pursuing a PhD represents the next essential step toward achieving these goals. Doctoral training will provide advanced expertise in epidemiologic methods, implementation science, advanced statistical analysis, interdisciplinary collaboration, and scientific leadership.

More importantly, it will enable me to design rigorous research that addresses complex public health problems, secures competitive research funding, and informs national and global health policies. I am particularly interested in working with internationally recognized research groups whose expertise aligns with my interests in HPV vaccination, climate change and health, maternal and child health, adolescent health, and health equity.

My Mission

Ultimately, my goal is to generate research that not only advances scientific knowledge but also improves the lives of individuals and communities. I believe that meaningful public health research should lead to practical solutions, equitable health policies, and stronger health systems.

Through continued collaboration, innovation, and lifelong learning, I hope to contribute to a healthier, more resilient, and more equitable future for populations worldwide.
