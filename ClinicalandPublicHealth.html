My professional journey combines clinical medicine, public health training, research, and humanitarian health practice, providing me with a multidisciplinary perspective on population health. My clinical background has enabled me to understand health problems at the individual level, while my public health training and field experience have strengthened my ability to examine these challenges through epidemiological, health-system, behavioral, and policy perspectives. This combination continues to shape my research interests and my aspiration to pursue doctoral training in public or global health.

01 — Education

Medical Training

I completed my Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) at Sir Salimullah Medical College, Bangladesh, where I developed a strong foundation in clinical medicine, preventive healthcare, and patient-centered care. My clinical training included exposure to medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, pediatrics, community medicine, and other major clinical disciplines.

Clinical practice taught me that many health outcomes cannot be addressed through treatment alone. Factors such as socioeconomic conditions, health literacy, vaccination access, gender, environment, health-seeking behavior, and the organization of healthcare services frequently determine whether individuals receive timely and effective care.

These experiences motivated my transition toward population-based approaches to health and ultimately led me to pursue advanced training in public health.

02 — Advanced Training

Public Health Training

I subsequently completed a Master of Public Health (MPH) at the Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP), strengthening my knowledge and skills in epidemiology, biostatistics, health systems, community health, research methodology, health policy, and program implementation.

My MPH training broadened my perspective from individual patient care to population health and strengthened my interest in generating evidence to guide public health programs and policies. It also provided a foundation for conducting quantitative and qualitative research and interpreting health data to understand the determinants of disease and health inequities.

07 — Research Perspective

From Clinical Practice to Research

My transition from clinical medicine to public health research has been driven by a fundamental question:

How can we move from treating individual illness to preventing disease and improving health at the population level?

Clinical practice taught me to understand the experiences and needs of individual patients. Public health training taught me to investigate patterns and determinants of health across populations. My humanitarian and field experience has shown me how social, environmental, behavioral, and health-system factors interact in real-world settings.

Clinical Medicine

Understanding individual patients, clinical conditions, preventive care, and patient-centered healthcare.

Public Health Training

Developing epidemiological, statistical, research, health systems, and policy perspectives.

Humanitarian Field Experience

Public Health Research

Moving toward evidence generation, implementation, evaluation, and policy-relevant research.

Together, these experiences have shaped my research perspective and strengthened my commitment to evidence-based public health.
